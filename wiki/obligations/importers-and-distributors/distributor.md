# Summary

A distributor is any natural or legal person in the supply chain, other than the manufacturer or the importer, who makes a product with digital elements available on the EU market.[^cra-art-3] Article 17 establishes their due care and verification responsibilities.[^cra-art-17]

# Legal effect

Distributors must act with due care in relation to CRA requirements. They are prohibited from making non-compliant products available and must cooperate with authorities and upstream operators to remedy identified cybersecurity risks.[^cra-art-17]

# Applicability

Applies to wholesalers, retailers, resellers, and digital marketplaces making products with digital elements available to EU end-users or other distributors.[^cra-art-17]

# Requirements or coverage

Before making a product available on the market, distributors must verify that:

1. **CE mark present:** The product bears the CE marking (Article 17(2));
2. **Accompanying documents:** The product is accompanied by required documentation, user instructions, and declaration of conformity (or internet link) in appropriate language;
3. **Traceability:** The manufacturer and importer have indicated their names, registered trade names/trademarks, and contact addresses on the product or packaging;
4. **Storage & transport safety:** Handling and storage conditions do not jeopardize compliance with Annex I requirements (Article 17(3));
5. **Action on suspected non-compliance:** If a distributor knows or has reason to believe a product is not in conformity, it must not make the product available until brought into conformity, and must inform the manufacturer or importer and market surveillance authorities (Article 17(2), (4));
6. **Corrective measures:** Cooperate with manufacturers and authorities in taking corrective action, withdrawals, or recalls (Article 17(4)).[^cra-art-17]

# Dates and transitions

- Applies from 11 December 2027.
