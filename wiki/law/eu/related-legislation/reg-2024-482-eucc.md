# Summary

Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2024/482 establishes the European Common Criteria-based cybersecurity certification scheme (EUCC), the first European scheme adopted under the Cybersecurity Act (Regulation (EU) 2019/881).[^reg-2024-482] It has been amended by Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2024/3144[^reg-2024-3144] (aligning ISO/IEC 15408:2022 and ISO/IEC 18045:2022 transition rules) and Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2025/2462[^reg-2025-2462] (introducing "product series" certification, defining "minor and major changes" for patch and assurance continuity, and updating state-of-the-art documents in Annexes I–III). EUCC provides a harmonised, Union-wide certification mechanism for ICT products, product series, and Protection Profiles across evaluation assurance levels 'substantial' and 'high'.[^reg-2024-482][^reg-2025-2462] Under the Cyber Resilience Act, EUCC certificates serve as recognised evidence for presumption of conformity and can fulfill mandatory third-party assessment requirements for important and critical products.[^cra-art-27][^cra-art-32]

# Interaction and scope relationship

EUCC is a voluntary certification scheme under the Cybersecurity Act. However, the Cyber Resilience Act establishes explicit statutory bridges that allow manufacturers to leverage EUCC certification to satisfy mandatory CRA conformity requirements.[^cra-art-32]

# When the instrument applies

Regulation (EU) 2024/482 applies whenever:
- A manufacturer or developer applies to an accredited and notified Conformity Assessment Body (ITSEF / Certification Body) for EUCC product certification, product series certification, or Protection Profile certification;[^reg-2024-482][^reg-2025-2462]
- Products such as smartcards, hardware security modules, operating systems, network devices, and secure microcontrollers are evaluated against Common Criteria Security Targets;[^reg-2024-482]
- Certified products undergo vulnerability monitoring, minor/major change assessments, and patch management under EUCC assurance continuity rules.[^reg-2024-482][^reg-2025-2462]

# Exclusion or concurrency with CRA

The instruments operate concurrently. Obtaining an EUCC certificate does not exempt a product from the CRA or the duty to affix the CE marking and issue an EU declaration of conformity. Instead, EUCC acts as an approved technical assessment route within the CRA framework.[^cra-art-27][^cra-art-32]

# Technical or procedural satisfaction of CRA elements

1. **Presumption of conformity via CRA delegated acts:** Under CRA Article 27(8) and (9), the Commission is empowered to adopt delegated acts (in accordance with Article 61) specifying which requirements of CRA Annex I are covered by EUCC certificates. Where such acts are adopted, products holding valid EUCC certificates benefit from a legal presumption of conformity with those CRA essential requirements.[^cra-art-27]
2. **Satisfaction of third-party conformity routes:** Under CRA Article 32(3) and (4), for important products with digital elements (Annex III Class I and Class II) and critical products (Annex IV), presenting a valid EUCC certificate at assurance level 'substantial' or 'high' fulfills the third-party conformity assessment obligation for the covered essential requirements, removing the need for a duplicate CRA Module B+C or Module H assessment.[^cra-art-32]
3. **Evaluation rigour, product series, and vulnerability testing:** EUCC penetration testing and vulnerability analysis methodologies (AVA_VAN) and the product-series and minor/major change rules introduced by Implementing Regulation (EU) 2025/2462 provide technical verification aligning with CRA Annex I Part I (security by design, attack resistance), Annex I Part II (vulnerability handling), and substantial modification management.[^reg-2024-482][^reg-2025-2462]

# Relevant provisions

- **Implementing Regulation (EU) 2024/482 (as amended by 2024/3144 and 2025/2462):** Article 1 (Subject matter and scope), Article 2 (Definitions, including 'product series', 'minor change', and 'major change'), Article 3 (Assurance levels 'substantial' and 'high'), Article 7 (Certificates and statements of conformity), Annexes I–III (State-of-the-art documents, protection profiles, and evaluation rules).[^reg-2024-482][^reg-2024-3144][^reg-2025-2462]
- **Regulation (EU) 2024/2847:** Article 27(8)–(9) (Presumption of conformity from CSA schemes via delegated acts), Article 32(3)–(4) (Conformity assessment routes for important and critical products), Recital 58.[^cra-art-27][^cra-art-32]

# Dates and transition

- Regulation (EU) 2024/482 was adopted on 31 January 2024 and entered into application on 27 February 2025.[^reg-2024-482]
- Implementing Regulation (EU) 2024/3144 was adopted on 18 December 2024, and Implementing Regulation (EU) 2025/2462 was adopted on 8 December 2025.[^reg-2024-3144][^reg-2025-2462]
- Existing national SOG-IS Common Criteria certificates may be transferred or recognized under the transitional provisions of Regulation (EU) 2024/482 Article 49.[^reg-2024-482][^reg-2024-3144]
- CRA recognition of EUCC certificates for presumption of conformity takes effect on 11 December 2027 (with delegated act empowerment active from 11 December 2024).[^cra-art-27]
