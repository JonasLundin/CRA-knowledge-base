# Summary

`EN 40000-1-1` (*Cybersecurity requirements for products with digital elements — Part 1-1: Vocabulary*, developed as `prEN 40000-1-1` / `FprEN 40000-1-1`) is the foundational horizontal terminology standard developed by CEN-CLC/JTC 13 WG 9 in support of Standardisation Request M/606[^m606][^stan4cra-jtc13]. Following approval in the CEN Formal Vote closing on 1 October 2026 and formal CEN-CENELEC ratification on 2 November 2026 (with target availability on 18 November 2026), it establishes a unified set of normative definitions, concepts, and terminology referenced across all horizontal (`EN 40000-1-2`, `prEN 40000-1-3`, `prEN 40000-1-4`, `CEN/CLC/TR 40000-1-5`) and vertical European Standards developed for Regulation (EU) 2024/2847[^cen-wi-81335].

# Legal effect

Although formally ratified as a European Standard (`EN 40000-1-1`), CEN-CENELEC project records indicate **no citation expected** in the Official Journal of the European Union (OJEU) for this vocabulary part[^cen-wi-81335]. Because it defines shared terminology rather than standalone technical or procedural conformity requirements, it confers **no** standalone legal presumption of conformity under CRA Article 27; instead, its normative definitions operate through the horizontal and vertical standards that normatively reference it.

# Applicability

Applies horizontally across all European Standards in the `EN 40000` family and vertical product-specific standards referencing the horizontal framework, as well as manufacturers, notified bodies, and market surveillance authorities applying CRA Annex I terminology.

# Requirements and high-level coverage

`prEN 40000-1-1` harmonises definitions across international cybersecurity standards (ISO/IEC, IEC 62443, ETSI) and CRA statutory definitions (Article 3), covering:
- Product boundary, intended purpose, operational environment, and reasonably foreseeable use / misuse;
- Asset, threat, vulnerability, exploitability, attack surface, and security risk;
- Cybersecurity-by-design, cybersecurity-by-default, and secure state;
- Lifecycle stages, support period, security update, and vulnerability handling roles;
- Software Bill of Materials (SBOM), third-party components, and coordinated vulnerability disclosure (CVD) terminology.
