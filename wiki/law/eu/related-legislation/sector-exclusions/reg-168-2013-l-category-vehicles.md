# Summary

Regulation (EU) No 168/2013 establishes the EU type-approval framework for L-category vehicles, including mopeds, motorcycles, tricycles, and quadricycles (categories L1e through L7e) and their systems, components, and separate technical units.[^reg-168-2013] Unlike passenger cars and heavy vehicles (which are directly excluded under CRA Article 2(2)(d)), L-category vehicles are subject to a conditional exclusion mechanism governed by CRA Article 2(5) and implemented via Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2025/1535.[^cra-art-2][^reg-2025-1535]

# Interaction and scope relationship

The relationship between Regulation (EU) No 168/2013 and the CRA is structured through a dynamic delegation mechanism:
- Because Regulation (EU) No 168/2013 did not initially incorporate comprehensive UN cybersecurity type-approval requirements (UN Regulations No 155 and No 156) across all subcategories, L-category vehicles were not excluded unconditionally at primary legislative level.[^cra-art-2]
- CRA Article 2(5) empowers the Commission to adopt delegated acts to exclude specific L-category vehicles, systems, or components once equivalent cybersecurity requirements are established under Regulation (EU) No 168/2013.[^cra-art-2]

# When the instrument applies

Regulation (EU) No 168/2013 applies whenever:
- L-category vehicles (e.g., electric mopeds, high-powered motorcycles, quadricycles) are manufactured, type-approved, and placed on the EU market;[^reg-168-2013]
- In-vehicle electronic control systems, digital instrument clusters, or anti-tampering powertrain software are assessed by national type-approval authorities;[^reg-168-2013]
- Type-approval compliance is verified by designated technical services.[^reg-168-2013]

# Exclusion or concurrency with CRA

The exclusion is conditional and determined by secondary legislation:[^cra-art-2]
- **Excluded vehicles:** L-category vehicles and components specifically identified in Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2025/1535 as subject to equivalent cybersecurity type-approval standards are exempt from the CRA.[^reg-2025-1535]
- **Retained in CRA:** L-category vehicles or digital systems not covered by equivalent cybersecurity type-approval rules, as well as aftermarket digital accessories, remain within the horizontal scope of the CRA.[^cra-art-2]

# Technical or procedural satisfaction of CRA elements

1. **Equivalence assessment under Article 2(5):** The Commission evaluates whether the cybersecurity rules applied under Regulation (EU) No 168/2013 provide a level of protection equivalent to CRA Annex I essential requirements.[^cra-art-2]
2. **Implementation via Delegated Regulation (EU) 2025/1535:** The Commission adopted Delegated Regulation (EU) 2025/1535 to formally specify the precise exclusions, harmonising the treatment of advanced L-category vehicles with motor vehicles under Regulation (EU) 2019/2144.[^reg-2025-1535]

# Relevant provisions

- **Regulation (EU) No 168/2013:** Article 2 (Scope), Article 4 (Vehicle categories L1e–L7e), Article 21 (General requirements on safety).[^reg-168-2013]
- **Regulation (EU) 2024/2847:** Article 2(5) (Empowerment for delegated acts on L-category vehicles), Recital 23.[^cra-art-2]
- **Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2025/1535:** Exclusion of specified L-category products from CRA scope.[^reg-2025-1535]

# Dates and transition

- Regulation (EU) No 168/2013 applied from 1 January 2016.[^reg-168-2013]
- CRA Article 2(5) empowerment took effect on 11 December 2024, enabling the adoption of Delegated Regulation (EU) 2025/1535 prior to full CRA application.[^cra-art-2][^reg-2025-1535]
