## 2. Executive Summary & Regulatory Context

On **11 September 2026**, the European Union Cyber Resilience Act (Regulation (EU) 2024/2847) achieved its first major mandatory milestone: the statutory vulnerability and incident reporting obligations set forth in **Article 14** officially took effect across all 27 EU Member States and the European Economic Area (Article 71(2)(a)).

Simultaneously:
1. **ENISA launched the live Single Reporting Platform (SRP):** Accessible at `https://portal.cra-srp.enisa.europa.eu`, establishing the centralized EU interface through which manufacturers and authorized representatives submit mandatory notifications to coordinator CSIRTs and ENISA.
2. **European Commission updated the CRA Implementation FAQ to Version 1.4:** Published on 4 September 2026 across seven official thematic chapters, clarifying critical enforcement questions regarding telemetry-driven awareness triggers, zero-days, products placed on the market prior to December 2027, and third-party component reachability.
3. **Commission Guidance C(2026) 5252 final was formalized:** Clarifying Section 9.1 reporting mechanics, the "sufficient degree of certainty" awareness test, and open-source software stewardship.

Release **v0.2.0** of the CRA Knowledge Base represents an end-to-end reconciliation of all normative sources, operational guides, statutory timelines, and conceptual definitions to align perfectly with the live Article 14 regulatory reality.

  - Actively Exploited Vulnerabilities (Article 14(2)(d)): Corrected deadline to **no later than 14 days after a corrective or mitigating measure is in place** (superseding previous general references to 1 month).
  - Severe Incidents (Article 14(4)(c)): Confirmed deadline is **within 1 month after submission of the 72-hour notification**.
  - Corrected in [`article-14-reporting.md`](file:///Users/jonaslundin/CRA-knowledge-base/wiki/obligations/reporting/article-14-reporting.md) and [`final-report-one-month.md`](file:///Users/jonaslundin/CRA-knowledge-base/wiki/obligations/reporting/final-report-one-month.md).

### Security & Compliance
- **Awareness Standard ("Sufficient Degree of Certainty"):** Clarified that the statutory 24-hour clock begins only after initial internal triage establishes verified knowledge that an actively exploited flaw affects the manufacturer's specific product.
- **Recital 68 Exclusion:** Confirmed that ethical security research, security audits, Coordinated Vulnerability Disclosure (CVD), and laboratory proof-of-concept (PoC) exploits do not constitute active exploitation and must NOT be reported under Article 14.
- **Third-Party Component Reachability:** Documented that upstream dependency vulnerabilities only trigger manufacturer reporting if the flaw is reachable, exploitable, and actively exploited within the context of the manufacturer's product.
- **Unvalidated Account Quota:** Documented the 20-notification cap for newly registered Assigned Representatives awaiting formal CSIRT Designated as Coordinator (CDaC) identity verification.

## 5. Implementation & Operational Guidance (Downstream Impact)

### 5.1 Product Security Incident Response Teams (PSIRT)
1. **Bookmark the Live SRP Endpoint:** Direct reporting personnel to `https://portal.cra-srp.enisa.europa.eu`. Ensure all potential reporters possess active **EU Login** accounts with multi-factor authentication (MFA) configured.
2. **Do Not Pre-Register Without an Incident:** Adhere to ENISA's explicit recommendation: do not create speculative manufacturer profiles on the SRP ahead of time. Complete registration concurrently with the first required early warning submission.
3. **Calibrate Triage SLA to 24/7 Running Clock:** Once initial triage confirms active exploitation in a reachable product component, the 24-hour clock runs continuously, including weekends and public holidays.
4. **Isolate Ethical CVD from Regulatory Reporting:** Ensure internal security vulnerability intake channels (e.g., bug bounties, HackerOne/Bugcrowd reports, security researcher emails) are not automatically routed to the SRP unless active, unauthorized in-the-wild exploitation is verified.

### 5.2 Engineering & Architecture Teams
1. **Third-Party Component Reachability Analysis:** Instrument Software Bills of Materials (SBOMs) with call-graph and reachability analysis tools to immediately assess whether upstream CVEs are executable in production deployments.
2. **Automated M2M Deferral:** Discontinue efforts to build direct REST/webhook integrations to the SRP for the September 2026 launch. Implement internal automated alerting that stages the 43 standardized fields for manual submission via the web portal.

### 5.3 Legal & Regulatory Compliance
1. **Retroactive Reporting Audit:** Verify that all products with digital elements currently supported on the EU market—even if placed on the market before 11 December 2027—are monitored for Article 14 reportable events.
2. **Authorized Representative Mandates:** Ensure non-EU manufacturers have established formal written agreements with EU Authorized Representatives under Article 20, as lead CSIRT routing defaults to the AR's Member State of establishment.
