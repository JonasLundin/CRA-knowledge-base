# Summary

The *Official Legislative Summary of Regulation (EU) 2024/2847* published on EUR-Lex provides an authoritative plain-language overview of the Cyber Resilience Act (CRA) [^eurlex-cra-summary]. It summarizes the legislative objectives, scope of application, key actors, essential cybersecurity requirements, reporting duties, conformity assessment framework, and market surveillance rules.

# Legal effect

This summary is an official informational tool prepared by the Publications Office of the European Union [^eurlex-cra-summary]. It is non-binding and carries no legislative authority. Only the authentic text of Regulation (EU) 2024/2847 as published in the Official Journal has legal force.

# Applicability

Provides a foundational reference for all stakeholders seeking an executive understanding of the CRA regulatory architecture.

# Key topics or guidance coverage

The summary outlines the core elements of the CRA [^eurlex-cra-summary]:

- **Objective and Scope:** Creating horizontal Union cybersecurity rules for hardware and software products with digital elements placed on the internal market, addressing the proliferation of cyber vulnerabilities and inadequate security update lifecycles.
- **Two Core Pillars:**
  1. *Cybersecurity by Design (Annex I Part I):* Ensuring products are engineered securely, without known exploitable vulnerabilities, configured securely by default, and capable of secure updates.
  2. *Vulnerability Handling (Annex I Part II):* Enforcing transparent vulnerability disclosure, rapid remediation, provision of free security updates, and active testing throughout the product support lifecycle.
- **Economic Operator Responsibilities:** Defining clear duties for manufacturers, authorized representatives, importers, distributors, and open-source software stewards.
- **Conformity Assessment & CE Marking:** Establishing internal control and third-party notified body assessment pathways based on product risk classification (Default, Important Class I & II, Critical).
- **Mandatory Reporting Mechanism:** Establishing early warning and reporting pathways for actively exploited vulnerabilities and severe incidents via the ENISA Single Reporting Platform.
- **Governance and Sanctions:** Coordination among market surveillance authorities (ADCO-CRA) and administrative fines up to €15 million or 2.5% of total worldwide annual turnover.

# Dates and transitions

- **Entry into Force:** 10 December 2024 (20 days after OJEU publication).
- **Notified Body Provisions:** Applied 11 June 2026 (18 months post-entry).
- **Reporting Obligations (Article 14):** Applies 11 September 2026 (21 months post-entry).
- **Full General Application:** Applies 11 December 2027 (36 months post-entry).
