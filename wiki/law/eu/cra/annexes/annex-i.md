# Summary

Annex I contains the substantive core of the Cyber Resilience Act, defining essential cybersecurity requirements across two distinct parts: product properties (Part I) and vulnerability handling lifecycle (Part II).

# Legal effect

Sets legally binding technical and organizational requirements that all products with digital elements must satisfy to be placed on the Union market.

# Applicability

Applies to manufacturers of all products with digital elements within scope of Regulation (EU) 2024/2847.

# Requirements or coverage

Annex I is structured in two parts:
- **Part I: Security requirements relating to the properties of products with digital elements**:
  - Delivered without known exploitable vulnerabilities;
  - Designed, developed, and produced to ensure an appropriate level of cybersecurity based on risk assessment;
  - Secure by default configuration, including reset capability to original state;
  - Protection from unauthorised access through robust authentication, identity, and access control;
  - Protection of data confidentiality (encryption at rest and in transit) and data integrity;
  - Attack surface minimisation and reduction of external interfaces;
  - Availability of essential functions and resilience against denial-of-service attacks;
  - Comprehensive logging and monitoring capabilities of security-relevant events;
  - Provision of security updates that are separate from feature updates and applied automatically by default where feasible.
- **Part II: Vulnerability handling requirements**:
  - Systematic identification and documentation of vulnerabilities and components (including SBOM);
  - Rapid remediation through security updates provided without delay and free of charge;
  - Effective testing and security reviews throughout the support period;
  - Public disclosure policy and coordinated vulnerability disclosure (CVD);
  - Secure distribution mechanisms for patches, including cryptographic verification of updates.

# Dates and transitions

Applies from 11 December 2027.
