# Summary

Regulation (EU) 2019/1020 provides the horizontal market surveillance framework across the European Single Market, setting common rules on authority powers, economic operator obligations (including EU-established responsible persons), cross-border mutual assistance, border controls, and information exchange through the ICSMS database.[^reg-2019-1020] Under CRA Article 67, the Cyber Resilience Act is added to Annex I of Regulation (EU) 2019/1020, establishing that CRA enforcement operates within this horizontal infrastructure.[^cra-art-67]

# Interaction and scope relationship

Regulation (EU) 2019/1020 applies concurrently with the Cyber Resilience Act. While CRA Chapter V (Articles 52 to 63) defines cybersecurity-specific surveillance procedures, corrective measures, and penalty structures, the underlying investigative powers, administrative assistance mechanisms, and customs controls derive from Regulation (EU) 2019/1020.[^cra-art-52]

# When the instrument applies

Regulation (EU) 2019/1020 applies whenever:
- Market surveillance authorities (MSAs) designated under CRA monitor, evaluate, or investigate products with digital elements placed or made available on the EU market;[^cra-art-52]
- Products are offered for sale online or through distance sales to EU end-users (triggering Article 4 responsible operator and Article 6 online sales rules);[^reg-2019-1020]
- MSAs cooperate across Member State borders, request peer assistance, or participate in coordinated joint market surveillance actions under Articles 22–24;[^reg-2019-1020]
- Customs authorities control products entering the EU market under Articles 25–28.[^reg-2019-1020]

# Exclusion or concurrency with CRA

The instruments apply concurrently. There is no mutual exclusion. CRA acts as lex specialis for cybersecurity-specific product risks and evaluation procedures (e.g., source code examination or vulnerability testing under CRA Article 54), while Regulation (EU) 2019/1020 governs the general market surveillance framework.[^cra-art-52]

# Technical or procedural satisfaction of CRA elements

1. **EU-established economic operator:** Under Article 4 of Regulation (EU) 2019/1020, products within CRA scope cannot be placed on the EU market unless an economic operator established in the Union (manufacturer, importer, authorized representative, or fulfilment service provider) is identified to cooperate with authorities.[^reg-2019-1020]
2. **ICSMS integration:** MSAs enter CRA compliance checks, non-compliance findings, risk evaluations, and corrective orders into the Information and Communication System on Market Surveillance (ICSMS) pursuant to Article 34 of Regulation (EU) 2019/1020 and CRA Article 54.[^cra-art-52]
3. **Customs and border controls:** Border control authorities use Regulation (EU) 2019/1020 Articles 25–28 to suspend release for free circulation if a product with digital elements lacks CE marking, EU declaration of conformity, or presents a serious cybersecurity risk.[^reg-2019-1020]
4. **Coordinated actions and ADCO:** CRA market surveillance authorities participate in the CRA Administrative Cooperation Group (ADCO) established under Regulation (EU) 2019/1020 Article 30 to harmonise national enforcement practices.[^cra-art-52]

# Relevant provisions

- **Regulation (EU) 2019/1020:** Article 4 (Tasks of economic operators), Articles 10–14 (Designation and powers of market surveillance authorities), Articles 22–24 (Mutual assistance and joint actions), Articles 25–28 (Controls on products entering the Union market), Article 34 (ICSMS).[^reg-2019-1020]
- **Regulation (EU) 2024/2847:** Articles 52–63 (Market surveillance and Union safeguard procedure), Article 67 (Amendment to Regulation (EU) 2019/1020 Annex I).[^cra-art-52][^cra-art-67]

# Dates and transition

- Regulation (EU) 2019/1020 entered into force on 16 July 2021 and is fully applicable across the EU.[^reg-2019-1020]
- CRA market surveillance obligations apply concurrently with the main entry into application of CRA on 11 December 2027.[^cra-art-52]
