# Summary

Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2024/482 establishes the European Common Criteria-based cybersecurity certification scheme (EUCC), the first European scheme adopted under the Cybersecurity Act (Regulation (EU) 2019/881).[^reg-2024-482] EUCC provides a harmonised, Union-wide certification mechanism for ICT products and Protection Profiles using the Common Criteria standards (ISO/IEC 15408 and ISO/IEC 18045) across evaluation assurance levels 'substantial' and 'high'.[^reg-2024-482] Under the Cyber Resilience Act, EUCC certificates serve as recognised evidence for presumption of conformity and can fulfill mandatory third-party assessment requirements for important and critical products.[^cra-art-27][^cra-art-32]

# Interaction and scope relationship

EUCC is a voluntary certification scheme under the Cybersecurity Act. However, the Cyber Resilience Act establishes explicit statutory bridges that allow manufacturers to leverage EUCC certification to satisfy mandatory CRA conformity requirements.[^cra-art-32]

# When the instrument applies

Regulation (EU) 2024/482 applies whenever:
- A manufacturer or developer applies to an accredited and notified Conformity Assessment Body (ITSEF / Certification Body) for EUCC product certification or Protection Profile certification;[^reg-2024-482]
- Products such as smartcards, hardware security modules, operating systems, network devices, and secure microcontrollers are evaluated against Common Criteria Security Targets;[^reg-2024-482]
- Certified products undergo vulnerability monitoring and patch management under EUCC maintenance rules.[^reg-2024-482]

# Exclusion or concurrency with CRA

The instruments operate concurrently. Obtaining an EUCC certificate does not exempt a product from the CRA or the duty to affix the CE marking and issue an EU declaration of conformity. Instead, EUCC acts as an approved technical assessment route within the CRA framework.[^cra-art-27][^cra-art-32]

# Technical or procedural satisfaction of CRA elements

1. **Presumption of conformity via CRA delegated acts:** Under CRA Article 27(8), the Commission is empowered to adopt delegated acts specifying which requirements of CRA Annex I are covered by EUCC certificates at assurance level 'substantial' or 'high'. Where such acts are adopted, products holding valid EUCC certificates benefit from a legal presumption of conformity with those CRA essential requirements.[^cra-art-27]
2. **Satisfaction of third-party conformity routes:** Under CRA Article 32(4), for important products with digital elements (Annex III Class I and Class II) and critical products (Annex IV), presenting a valid EUCC certificate at assurance level 'substantial' or 'high' fulfills the third-party conformity assessment obligation, removing the need for a duplicate CRA Module B+C or Module H assessment.[^cra-art-32]
3. **Evaluation rigour and vulnerability testing:** EUCC penetration testing and vulnerability analysis methodologies (AVA_VAN) provide technical verification aligning with CRA Annex I Part I (security by design, attack resistance) and Annex I Part II (vulnerability handling).[^reg-2024-482]

# Relevant provisions

- **Implementing Regulation (EU) 2024/482:** Article 1 (Subject matter and scope), Article 3 (Assurance levels 'substantial' and 'high'), Article 7 (Certificates and statements of conformity), Annex I (EUCC requirements and evaluation rules).[^reg-2024-482]
- **Regulation (EU) 2024/2847:** Article 27(8)–(9) (Presumption of conformity from CSA schemes), Article 32(4) (Conformity assessment routes for important products), Recital 58.[^cra-art-27][^cra-art-32]

# Dates and transition

- Regulation (EU) 2024/482 was adopted on 31 January 2024 and entered into application on 27 February 2025.[^reg-2024-482]
- Existing national SOG-IS Common Criteria certificates may be transferred or recognized under the transitional provisions of Regulation (EU) 2024/482 Article 19.[^reg-2024-482]
- CRA recognition of EUCC certificates for presumption of conformity takes effect on 11 December 2027.[^cra-art-27]
