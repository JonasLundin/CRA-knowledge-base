# Summary

The European Commission's *Open Source Software Guidance and Stewardship* clarifies the legal framework governing free and open-source software (FOSS) under Regulation (EU) 2024/2847 [^ec-cra-open-source]. It delineates the boundary between purely non-commercial community development and commercial placement on the market, while detailing the light-touch regime for open-source software stewards under Article 24.

# Legal effect

This guidance is non-binding and intended to provide legal certainty to the open-source ecosystem [^ec-cra-open-source]. It interprets Article 2, Article 3, Article 24, and Recitals 18–20 of Regulation (EU) 2024/2847.

# Applicability

Applies to individual open-source developers, foundations, hosting platforms, non-profit open-source software stewards, and commercial entities integrating FOSS components into commercial products.

# Key topics or guidance coverage

The guidance addresses the open-source governance framework under the CRA [^ec-cra-open-source]:

- **Non-Commercial Exemption (Article 2 & Recitals 18–19):** Software developed or supplied outside the course of a commercial activity is excluded from the scope of the CRA. The mere hosting of code on public repositories, receipt of donations, or participation by employed developers does not automatically make the activity commercial.
- **Commercial Activity Criteria (Recital 20):** Identifies factors indicating commercial placement, such as charging for the software, offering monetised technical support or commercial warranties, or integrating software into a monetised proprietary offering.
- **Open-Source Software Stewards (Article 24):** Defined as legal persons (such as foundations or associations) providing sustained support for the development of open-source software intended for commercial activities without being manufacturers themselves.
- **Steward Obligations (Article 24(1)–(3)):** Stewards must put in place a cybersecurity policy, ensure coordinated vulnerability disclosure, cooperate with market surveillance authorities, and facilitate sharing of vulnerability information. Stewards are exempt from CE marking, technical files, and full conformity assessment.
- **Voluntary Reporting (Article 14(8)):** Open-source stewards and non-commercial developers may voluntarily notify actively exploited vulnerabilities and severe incidents to national CSIRTs and ENISA via the Single Reporting Platform.

# Dates and transitions

- **Application Timeline:** Article 24 obligations apply from the general application date of 11 December 2027.
