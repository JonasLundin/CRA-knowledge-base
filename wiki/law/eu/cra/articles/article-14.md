# Summary

Article 14 establishes binding notification obligations and strict timeframes for manufacturers experiencing actively exploited vulnerabilities or severe cybersecurity incidents.

# Legal effect

Imposes statutory notification duties to the designated CSIRT coordinator and ENISA, and mandates communication to affected users.

# Applicability

Applies to all manufacturers of products with digital elements placed on the Union market.

# Requirements or coverage

Article 14 establishes a phased notification procedure:
- **Triggers**:
  1. Any actively exploited vulnerability contained in the product with digital elements;
  2. Any severe incident having an impact on the security of the product.
- **Tiered reporting timeline**:
  - **Early warning**: Within 24 hours of becoming aware, indicating whether exploitation is suspected of being unlawful or malicious;
  - **Vulnerability / Incident notification**: Within 72 hours of becoming aware, providing initial findings, sensitivity, and general risk mitigation measures;
  - **Final report**: Within 14 days after a corrective measure or mitigation is available (for vulnerabilities), or no later than 1 month after incident handling (for severe incidents).
- **Routing**: Notifications are submitted simultaneously to the designated CSIRT coordinator of the Member State where the manufacturer is established and to ENISA via the Single Reporting Platform (Article 16).
- **User notification**: Manufacturers must inform affected users without undue delay about the incident or vulnerability and any available corrective measures.

# Dates and transitions

Applies from 11 September 2026 (earlier than general CRA application date of 11 December 2027).
