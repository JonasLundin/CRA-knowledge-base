# Summary

Directive (EU) 2020/1828 establishes a harmonised procedural regime enabling designated qualified entities (such as national consumer protection associations) to bring representative actions before courts or administrative authorities to obtain injunctive measures and redress (e.g., compensation, repair, replacement, or contract termination) for infringements of Union consumer protection law.[^dir-2020-1828] Cyber Resilience Act Article 69 amends Annex I of Directive (EU) 2020/1828 to include Regulation (EU) 2024/2847, granting consumers collective judicial remedies against CRA non-compliance.[^cra-art-69]

# Interaction and scope relationship

Directive (EU) 2020/1828 operates concurrently with the public administrative enforcement and market surveillance mechanisms of the CRA. While market surveillance authorities enforce CRA through fines, market withdrawals, and administrative orders under Chapter V, Directive (EU) 2020/1828 provides a parallel civil litigation avenue for collective consumer harm.[^dir-2020-1828][^cra-art-69]

# When the instrument applies

Directive (EU) 2020/1828 applies whenever:
- A qualified entity designated by a Member State initiates a representative action against a trader (e.g., manufacturer, importer, distributor) for alleged violations of Union law harming collective consumer interests;[^dir-2020-1828]
- Violations involve consumer products with digital elements placed or made available on the EU market;[^cra-art-69]
- Remedies sought include injunctive orders (stopping unlawful practices) or redress orders (compensating affected consumers).[^dir-2020-1828]

# Exclusion or concurrency with CRA

The two instruments apply concurrently. The inclusion of the CRA in Annex I of Directive (EU) 2020/1828 confirms that CRA manufacturer obligations directly benefit consumers and can be enforced through collective civil actions.[^cra-art-69]

# Technical or procedural satisfaction of CRA elements

1. **Collective enforcement of manufacturer duties:** Representative actions may be brought against manufacturers for systematic breaches of CRA obligations, such as:[^dir-2020-1828][^cra-art-69]
   - Premature termination of software security updates before the stated support period has elapsed (CRA Article 13);
   - Misleading or absent information regarding the guaranteed support period at the point of sale (CRA Article 13 and Annex II);
   - Placing insecure consumer IoT products on the market with known unpatched critical vulnerabilities (CRA Annex I).
2. **Redress mechanisms:** Where consumer devices become unusable, insecure, or suffer data loss due to CRA non-compliance, qualified entities can seek collective redress including free security patches, hardware replacement, price reduction, or financial restitution.[^dir-2020-1828]

# Relevant provisions

- **Directive (EU) 2020/1828:** Article 1 (Subject matter and purpose), Article 2 (Scope), Article 7 (Injunctive measures), Article 9 (Redress measures), Annex I (List of Union acts).[^dir-2020-1828]
- **Regulation (EU) 2024/2847:** Article 69 (Amendments to Directive (EU) 2020/1828), Recital 99.[^cra-art-69]

# Dates and transition

- Directive (EU) 2020/1828 entered into force on 24 December 2020 and has applied in Member States since 25 June 2023.[^dir-2020-1828]
- The inclusion of CRA under Annex I takes effect alongside the general application of the CRA on 11 December 2027.[^cra-art-69]
